## Why facilitation matters in quality

Most of what an organisation knows about its quality problems sits in people who rarely speak in meetings: testers, support agents, junior developers. Conventional meetings (a presentation, then "any questions?") let a few voices dominate and produce polite, incomplete answers.

[Liberating Structures](https://www.liberatingstructures.com/) are a repertoire of 43 simple interaction patterns that distribute participation: everyone thinks alone first, then in pairs, then in groups. I use them because they produce **better evidence**: concrete, from many roles, and gathered in a safe way.

## Which structure for which question

| Quality question | Structure | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| How do we decide what to test today? | 1-2-4-All | Everyone contributes before the loudest opinion lands |
| What would make quality fail? | TRIZ | Humour lowers defences; step 2 reveals what is already happening |
| Who really takes part in quality? | Social Network Webbing (adapted) | Makes visible who is missing from decisions |
| What does each area need from the other? | What I Need From You | Surfaces blocked communication without debate |
| Where are the contradictions in how we plan? | Wicked Questions | Names tensions without blaming anyone |
| Which metrics and reports still earn their place? | Ecocycle Planning | Shows what is kept out of habit |
| What must always be done for a test to be trustworthy? | Min Specs | Separates the essential rules from bureaucracy |
| What do our best testers do differently? | Appreciative Interviews | Starts from success, so people speak openly about gaps afterwards |
| What can we improve without asking permission? | 15% Solutions | Produces quick wins teams own |
| What did we learn, and what now? | What, So What, Now What? (W³) | Turns a session into next steps |

## How I run them online

- 6 to 10 participants, a 90-minute slot planned for 60 minutes. The spare time absorbs delays and conversations worth extending.
- A whiteboard (Canva, Miro or similar) with one section per activity and pre-assigned columns per participant.
- Breakout rooms tested beforehand with the organiser account; groups defined in advance.
- A visible **parking lot** for topics outside the session's goal.
- Ground rules read and agreed at the start: Chatham House rule, describe what happens today rather than what the procedure says, nobody is evaluated, the facilitator may cut a conversation to protect time.
- Recording or transcription only with explicit consent. By default I take notes.
- Direct managers do not join sessions with operational staff.
